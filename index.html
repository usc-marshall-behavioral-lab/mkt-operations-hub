Rules That Never Bend

Rules that never bend
  • No names in the Google Doc spreadsheet. IDs only. Anything with a name on it stays on paper.
  • One in-person session per student. Anyone who has already done one cannot participate.
  • Doors close at 5 after. No late entry.
  • Never enter answers for a previous participant. Close the study and clear the cache instead.

Roles & Responsibilities

Marketing (MKT) Research Participation for BUAD 307

PhD Supervisor
  • Coordinates the session: study setup, link creation, study order and rotation, filler studies, and the physical sign-up sheet (printed by the PhD supervisor or the Lead RA).
  • Collects study information by Thursday the week before and specifies the order for each session in the Google Doc spreadsheet.
  • Posts all credit by the end of each session day.
Research Assistants
  • Handle day-of operations: arrive 30 minutes early, power on all computers, set up through School View, prepare post-its and materials, run check-in, monitor participants, document issues in the Google Doc spreadsheet, and manage the end of the session.
  • Keep the email and phone number of the PhD supervisor in charge that week.

Lead RA: The person who performed setup. If the Lead RA leaves early, the assistant RA who started the day takes over as Lead for remaining sessions.

Card Access & Building Policy

Per Provost building policy, all card access to VPD expires at the end of each semester. This affects undergraduates, master’s students, and doctoral students; access must be reactivated each semester.

The lab confirms each semester who is and isn’t a current lab member and submits reactivation requests. If your access is not working at the start of a new semester, this expiration cycle is the most likely reason. Faculty and full-time staff are not affected; their access stays active continuously.

If your access is not working, contact Brian Huh right away (huhb@marshall.usc.edu, 213-821-9746) so it can be resolved before your session.

PhD Supervisor ReminderRemind your RAs to verify their access before the first session of each new semester.

First-time tap after reactivation (Schlage reader)

A newly reactivated card needs three long taps on the Schlage logo before it works; after that, a single tap works for the rest of the semester.

Tap 1: Hold card to the Schlage logo. Wait for the beep. Logo flashes red. Lift card.
Tap 2: Hold card to the logo again. Wait for the beep. Logo flashes red again. Lift card.
Tap 3: Hold card to the logo one more time. You’ll hear a beep and the logo turns green, and the door unlocks.

Study Preparation

PhD Supervisor
  • By Thursday the week before studies run, collect study information from the participating researchers.
  • Decide the order and rotation of studies and specify it for each session in the Google Doc spreadsheet.
  • Ask each participating researcher which browser their study uses (Chrome, Firefox, or Edge). Don’t assume Chrome; studies in the same session can need different browsers.
  • Create each study’s links in the browser it needs and put them in the study folder. See the separate Study Folder Setup & Distribution Guide.
  • Name links so they identify the study. The name can come from the researcher (e.g., matching their information sheet or consent form) or from the PhD supervisor. Never use the researcher’s name in a link.
  • Keep study materials stocked throughout the session so they don’t run out.
  • Provide post-its and pens if needed.
  • Print the physical sign-up sheet, or have the Lead RA print it.
Research Assistants
  • The PhD supervisor in charge that week creates the links, sets the study order, and notes it for each session in the Google Doc spreadsheet.
  • Check the Google Doc spreadsheet before each session so you know the order to use.
  • Confirm which browser (Chrome, Firefox, or Edge) each study uses, and open each study in that browser.
  • For how study folders are created and distributed to all computers, see the separate Study Folder Setup & Distribution Guide.

5 Minutes Before Start Time

Sessions start on the hour or the half hour (some days run 9:30, 10:30, 11:30…). Check each day’s start times on the Schedule.

Go outside and greet arriving participants. Make sure they are here for marketing, not management.

Say This“Are you here for the marketing study?”
“Please find the reminder email for this session. It has your 6-digit SONA ID, and you’ll need it to check in.”
“Please turn your phone off now. It goes in a phone locker when you check in.”
If they don’t have their IDAs a fallback they can give you their name and USC email (it must be their USC email, not another address), but the 6-digit ID is much easier.

At Start Time: Check-In Steps

Open the door and have participants enter one by one.
Ask for their 6-digit SONA ID.
Check them off the sign-up sheet.
Phone: verify it is completely powered off (not just silenced), place it in a phone locker, lock it, and give the participant the key. The key number is their seat number. At the end of the session, they hand the key to the RA and get their phone back.
Give them the post-it with their ID and ask them to put it somewhere on their station where they can see it.
Ask them to put their bags by the back wall. Water is allowed unless a study asks them not to have it; other drinks and food stay with their belongings in the back.
Send them to the station that matches their key number (stations are numbered). Hand out keys at random so seats alternate throughout the room. If they seem to have come with a friend, give them keys for stations that are not next to each other.
Tell them not to touch the computer or start anything yet. You may need to repeat this midway through check-in.
Say This“Please don’t touch the computer or start anything yet. Everyone will start together.”
Late-comers before the door closesThey follow the same steps, including the phone policy. After the door closes, see Doorbell & late arrivals.

Door Close Policy

Door close policyClose the door 5 minutes after start time, or when you finish checking people in, whichever is later.
Nobody is allowed into the session after the door closes.

Introduction Protocol

  • One person does the talking during the introduction while one person stays at the back.
  • Both people introduce themselves. When the announcement is over, the person at the back comes to the front of the room.
  • Tell participants how to get help: they raise the red numbered flag at their station.
  • Participants need to feel they have privacy. When you come to a station, be as unobtrusive as possible.
Say This“If you have a question or need anything during the session, raise the red numbered flag at your station and one of us will come to you.”

Full word-for-word opening: Session opening script.

Session Opening Script + Checklist

How to Use This Script One person does the talking (the Lead RA, at the front) while one person stays at the back. Both introduce themselves. Read the script below to participants. After the announcement, the person in back should come to the front of the room. It is important that participants feel they have privacy, so when you come to their station, be as unobtrusive as possible.
Lead RA

Thank you for participating in the marketing research studies. My name is [NAME of the person making the announcement].

Assistant RA

Hi, and I am [name of the person in the back of the room].

Lead RA

We will be taking you through a series of studies today.

Over the course of this session, you will be participating in several studies related to marketing and consumer behavior.

Lead RA: Pacing

Everyone will be here for the ENTIRE session, so please take your time with the studies. There's no sense rushing. Everybody has their own pace, and you will not be penalized if you do not finish all studies.

Lead RA: ID Instructions

You have a 6-digit ID written on the Post-it I handed you when you walked in. Each study will prompt you to write down or enter this number. Make sure to use THIS number and NOT your USC ID, because we need the 6-digit number to give you credit for taking all the studies today.

Lead RA: Tech

If you experience any technical difficulties with a study, or need anything during the session, raise the red numbered flag at your station and one of us will come to you.

Lead RA: Headphones (if applicable)

[If one of the studies requires headphones,] One of the studies requires using the headphones. We will let you know when to put on the headphones.

Lead RA: Study Order

There is a SET ORDER in which these studies are arranged. You will NOT be taking the studies in the order they are listed in the folder in front of you. The reason for that order is that it allows me to keep track that you were here and get credit.

We will all start the first study together, and then EVERY TIME you finish a study, raise your red flag, and one of us will come get you started on the next study.

Lead RA: Focus

It is really important that you do not engage in any tasks other than these studies during this session.

Lead RA: Questions

Are there any questions before we get started?

[Pause for questions. Address any concerns.]

Lead RA: Start

Ok, your first study is [NAME OF STUDY 1]. Please remember to raise your red flag when you are done.

Session Start Checklist
  • All participants have their 6-digit ID on Post-it
  • Headphones distributed (if applicable)
  • Participants ready to begin
  • Both RAs positioned and ready

Observation Techniques

  • Watch for raised red flags. The flag number tells you which station to go to.
  • Record participant ID numbers subtly, on a small notepad or in the Google Doc spreadsheet on the Proctor/RA Desk computer.
  • Don’t stare or make participants feel watched; use casual glances while walking around.
  • Try to notice when someone is no longer doing a study, but don’t rush them or interfere.
  • Position yourself where you can see multiple stations without being intrusive.
  • If a participant notices you watching, smile briefly and look away naturally.

Participant Questions & Red Flags

When a red flag goes up, walk over quietly. Answer at the station, keep your voice low, and have the participant lower the flag.

Question or situationResponse
Errors or typos in a studyNote it in the Google Doc spreadsheet and tell the PhD supervisor.
Doesn’t understand a questionWhatever they interpret it as is what they should base their response on. Note it in the Google Doc spreadsheet if it keeps coming up.
Doesn’t know a wordYou can tell them the meaning or they can use a dictionary. If they use a dictionary constantly, note it in the Google Doc spreadsheet.
Wants to use the restroomAsk if it is urgent. If so, let them go after they finish the current study. Only one person at a time.
Done earlyThey sit quietly at their station and may browse the web on their computer.

Filler Studies & Headphones

Filler studies
  • RAs should know which studies are fillers (non-scheduled studies).
  • Give fillers only to participants who finish before 50 minutes.
  • Never disclose to participants that a study is a filler.
Headphone studiesIf a study uses headphones, make sure participants take them off afterward and don’t keep using them (e.g., to listen to music).

  • Close the study and open it again.

Session Documentation

Accurate documentation is critical for research integrity. Your notes help PhD supervisors identify factors that may affect data validity, ensure correct credit, and improve future sessions.

  • The PhD supervisor shares the Google Doc spreadsheet URL for their sessions each week. If you don’t have it, contact the PhD supervisor right away.
  • Open it on the Proctor/RA Desk computer and keep it open for the whole session.
  • Document in real time when possible, discreetly, so participants don’t notice.
  • If real-time notes aren’t possible, write on paper and transfer everything to the Google Doc spreadsheet before you sign out.
Critical privacy ruleNo names in the Google Doc spreadsheet, ever. Use SONA ID numbers only. Without an ID, use a temporary number (e.g., 2222) and write “temp ID, actual ID to be confirmed” in the Notes/Issues column.

After the Session

End script · Credit posting · Excused absences

Jump to checklist

End-of-Session Script

If people are still working 5 minutes before the session ends, stop them individually:

Say This“You’ve done all the studies we needed today. Thank you!”

If they ask about the other studies in the folder:

Say This“We’re only running a subset of the studies in the folder today.”

Then, to the whole room:

Say This“Thank you all for participating and for helping with this research. Your credit will be posted by the end of the day.”

Each participant hands their locker key to the RA and gets their phone back. Make sure every key is returned, then let everybody go.

Then reset the room

  • Close all open study content, browser tabs, and survey windows on every station so screens are clear for the next group
  • Collect post-its from the stations
  • Make sure all 16 locker keys are back
  • Log out of SimpleTexting

Credit Posting & Follow-Up

Credit posting policyThe PhD supervisor posts all credit by the end of each session day. Do not defer to the end of the week.
  • People who participated without signing up: the PhD supervisor enrolls them and gives credit, or sends their name and USC email to Brian Huh to issue credit.
  • Temporary IDs (e.g., 2222): record the participant’s actual ID in the Google Doc spreadsheet. Ask Brian for it if necessary.
  • No-shows: mark as an excused absence. Do not use unexcused absences in the system.
  • Discrepancies: email anything unclear (participation status, what happened with a participant) to Brian Huh (huhb@marshall.usc.edu) with the session date, time, participant SONA ID, and a description of the issue.
Privacy noteIf a participant tells the PhD supervisor they will not attend, the PhD supervisor can assure them they will be marked excused. RAs do not need to know who will not show up, or who did not show up, for any reason.

What to Do If…

A walk-in arrives (not signed up for this session)

  • Ask whether they have done any in-person session. If they have, they cannot participate and would not get credit. Students may do only ONE in-person session in total.
  • Ask them to wait until everyone who signed up is checked in.
  • If there is space they can participate, but wait until the 5-minute mark before taking them over someone who signed up.
  • While they wait, they should find their 6-digit SONA ID. If they cannot log in to SONA to find it, assign a temporary ID such as 2222 and note it in the Google Doc spreadsheet.
  • At the end of the session, tell the PhD supervisor about the walk-in so they can give credit and record the actual ID.
Say This“Please wait here until everyone who signed up is checked in. If a seat opens, you can join.”
Privacy ruleNo names in the Google Doc spreadsheet.

Someone signed up for a different session that week

  • Same as a walk-in: they look up their 6-digit SONA ID and wait until 5 after to see if there is space.
  • If they participate in a different session than the one they signed up for, note it in the Google Doc spreadsheet. Some researchers want to know which session a person attended.

Someone has not registered with the marketing research system (SONA)

  • They cannot participate. They first need to complete registration.
  • They can come to a different session, even one an hour later, but they should not rush through registration on the spot.

Doorbell & Late Arrivals

Someone rings the video doorbell or knocks

  • The lab has a 2-way video doorbell. When someone rings, the monitor at the Proctor/RA Desk shows a live feed of the door. The doorbell rings silently inside the lab so the session is not disturbed.
  • Press the pre-recorded message button to play: “Please wait, someone will be with you shortly.” This lets you manage visitors without leaving the session or making noise.
  • One person always stays in charge of the session while the other checks the monitor and handles the door. Don’t ignore the doorbell; visitors will keep ringing or start knocking.
  • Keep a sign outside the door directing visitors to use the video doorbell instead of knocking.
  • Step-by-step version: Appendix B.

A participant arrives after the session has started

Late arrivals should ring the video doorbell rather than knock or try to enter. Play the pre-recorded message, then the RA watching the video feed steps outside and quietly explains.

Say This“The session has already started, so you can’t join this one. You’re welcome to sign up for a different session.”
Hard ruleDo not let anyone in after the door closes.

Aggressive or Threatening Behavior

  • Guide the person away from the door so the session is not disturbed.
  • Talk to them calmly.
  • If that doesn’t resolve it, send them to Brian Huh (program manager) in VPD LL110A.
  • If they won’t go, walk over and get Brian.
  • Brian has business cards for Professor Kristin Diehl, Subject Pool Faculty Director; any concerns they have should go to her.

Documentation is mandatoryFailing to document issues can compromise research validity and waste months of work. When you see something unusual, ask: “Could this affect how this participant responded?” If yes, document it. PhD supervisors rely on your observations when deciding which data to keep.

Best practices

  • Be specific: “Completed Study A in 2 min (typical is 8–10 min),” not just “rushed.”
  • Be objective: describe behavior, not judgments: “Clicked through without scrolling,” not “lazy participant.”
  • Include timing: note when issues happened.
  • Don’t skip minor issues: small things can affect data validity. When in doubt, document it.
  • Use station numbers: they help identify equipment problems (e.g., “Station 7: monitor flickering”).
  • Note patterns: if several participants struggle with the same question, document each occurrence.
Not sure whether to document something?Document it anyway and mention it to the PhD supervisor. It is better to over-document than to lose context about a possible data issue. Questions about documentation practice go to the PhD supervisor or Brian Huh.
